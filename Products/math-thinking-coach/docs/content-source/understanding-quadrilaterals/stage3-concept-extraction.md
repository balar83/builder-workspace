## Section 1 — Polygons: curves, convexity and regularity
- **Curve vs. polygon**: a polygon is a simple closed curve made entirely of line segments — not every closed shape qualifies (a circle is closed but not a polygon; it has no straight sides). *(§3.2)*
- **Simple**: the boundary does not cross itself. *(§3.2)*
- **Convex polygon**: every interior angle measures less than 180°; equivalently, every diagonal lies entirely inside the polygon. *(§3.2)*
- **Concave polygon**: has at least one interior angle greater than 180° (a "caved-in" vertex). *(§3.2)*
- **Regular polygon**: all sides equal in length AND all angles equal in measure — both conditions together, not just one. *(§3.2)*
- **Irregular polygon**: fails at least one of the two regularity conditions. *(§3.2)*

## Section 2 — Angle sum property of a polygon
- **Interior angle sum formula**: for a polygon with n sides, the sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°. *(§3.3)*
- **Exterior angle sum property**: the sum of the exterior angles of any convex polygon, taken one at each vertex in order, is always 360° — regardless of the number of sides. *(§3.3)*
- **Interior/exterior linear pair**: at any single vertex, the interior angle and its exterior angle add up to 180°. *(§3.3)*
- **Regular-polygon shortcuts**: for a regular n-gon, each exterior angle = 360°/n, and each interior angle = (n−2)×180°/n. *(§3.3)*

## Section 3 — Trapezium and kite
- **Trapezium**: a quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides (the "bases"); the two non-parallel sides are the "legs". A shape with both pairs of opposite sides parallel is classified as a parallelogram instead, not a trapezium, under the definition used in this course. *(§3.4)*
- **Kite**: a quadrilateral with two distinct pairs of adjacent (not opposite) sides equal in length. *(§3.4)*
- **Kite diagonal properties**: the diagonals intersect at right angles, and exactly one of the two diagonals is bisected by the other (unlike a parallelogram, where both diagonals bisect each other). *(§3.4)*

## Section 4 — Parallelograms and their special cases
- **Parallelogram**: opposite sides are parallel and equal in length; opposite angles are equal; adjacent (consecutive) angles are supplementary (sum to 180°); the diagonals bisect each other. *(§3.5)*
- **Rhombus**: a parallelogram with the extra condition that all four sides are equal. Its diagonals bisect each other at right angles, and each diagonal bisects the pair of vertex angles it passes through. *(§3.5)*
- **Rectangle**: a parallelogram with the extra condition that all four angles are 90°. Its diagonals are equal in length, in addition to bisecting each other. *(§3.5)*
- **Square**: satisfies both the rhombus condition (all sides equal) and the rectangle condition (all angles 90°) simultaneously — it is both a rhombus and a rectangle at once. Its diagonals are equal in length AND bisect each other at right angles. *(§3.5)*
- **Hierarchy (one-directional "is-a" relationships)**: every square is a rectangle; every square is a rhombus; every rectangle is a parallelogram; every rhombus is a parallelogram. None of these relationships reverse — e.g. not every rectangle is a square. *(§3.5, synthesized across the section)*
