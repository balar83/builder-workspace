# Stage 3 — Concept Extraction: Exponents and Powers

Extracted against the Stage 2 structure for `topic-exponents-and-powers-laws-and-standard-form` (Option A, 4 sections).

## Section 1 — Powers with negative exponents
- **Negative exponent rule**: for any non-zero integer a and positive integer m, a⁻ᵐ = 1/aᵐ. *(§10.2)*
- **Multiplicative inverse**: a⁻ᵐ is the multiplicative inverse of aᵐ (and vice versa), since aᵐ × a⁻ᵐ = a⁰ = 1. *(§10.2)*
- **Sign behaviour**: a negative base raised to a negative exponent is positive when the (positive) power is even, negative when it is odd — the sign is decided entirely by aᵐ before inverting, not by the exponent's own sign. *(§10.2, derived)*
- **Expanded form with negative powers of 10**: a decimal number's digits after the decimal point correspond to 10⁻¹, 10⁻², 10⁻³, ... in place-value order, extending the whole-number expanded-form pattern (10⁰, 10¹, 10², ...). *(§10.2)*

## Section 2 — Laws of exponents
- **Product law**: aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ, for any non-zero integer a and any integers m, n (extends the Class VII positive-exponent version). *(§10.3)*
- **Quotient law**: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ. *(§10.3)*
- **Power-of-a-power law**: (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ — useful for re-expressing a power with one base as a power with a different (related) base. *(§10.3)*
- **Product of same-exponent different bases**: aᵐ × bᵐ = (ab)ᵐ. *(§10.3)*
- **Quotient of same-exponent different bases**: aᵐ/bᵐ = (a/b)ᵐ, and its negative-exponent form (a/b)⁻ᵐ = (b/a)ᵐ. *(§10.3)*
- **Zero exponent**: a⁰ = 1 for any non-zero integer a — true regardless of how complicated the base expression is, as long as it's provably non-zero. *(§10.3)*
- **Solving for an unknown exponent**: if two powers with the same non-zero base (other than 1 or −1) are equal, their exponents must be equal — used to solve simple linear equations in the exponent. *(§10.3, Example 5)*
- **Mixed simplification**: multi-step problems combine two or more of the above laws in sequence; order of operations still applies (innermost brackets/powers first). *(§10.3, Examples 4 and 7)*

## Section 3 — Standard form for very small numbers
- **Standard form**: any positive number can be written as k × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ k < 10 and n is an integer; for very small numbers, n is negative. *(§10.4)*
- **Converting to standard form**: count how many places the decimal point must move right to leave exactly one non-zero digit before it — that count (negated) is the exponent. *(§10.4)*
- **Converting from standard form**: reverse the process — move the decimal point left by |n| places when n is negative. *(§10.4, Example 9)*

## Section 4 — Comparing and combining very large and very small numbers
- **Comparing via ratio**: dividing one standard-form number by another (with matching or adjusted powers of 10) shows how many times larger/smaller one quantity is than the other. *(§10.4.1)*
- **Adding/subtracting in standard form**: numbers must first be rewritten with the same power of 10 before their coefficients can be added or subtracted; the result is then renormalised back into standard form if needed. *(§10.4.1, "Mass of earth and moon" and "Sun–Earth–Moon distance" worked problems)*
