## Section 1 — Reading graphs you already know
- **Data**: information collected in the context of a situation being studied, organised systematically before interpretation. *(`dh-raw-01`)*
- **Pictograph**: pictorial representation where one symbol (or a fraction of one) stands for a fixed quantity. *(`dh-raw-01`)*
- **Bar graph**: bars of uniform width, equally spaced, with height proportional to the value represented. *(`dh-raw-01`)*
- **Double bar graph**: two data sets shown side-by-side per category, for direct comparison. *(`dh-raw-01`)*
- **Order-independence**: rearranging a bar graph's bars doesn't change the information it conveys, only its presentation. *(`dh-raw-01`, "Think, Discuss and Write")*

## Section 2 — Pie charts: showing parts of a whole
- **Circle graph / pie chart**: shows the relationship between a whole and its parts; the whole circle is divided into sectors sized proportionally to what they represent. *(`dh-raw-03`)*
- **Central angle formula**: a sector's central angle = (that part's fraction of the whole) × 360°. *(`dh-raw-03`)*
- **Construction method**: draw a circle, mark centre and a radius, use a protractor to mark each sector's angle in turn. *(`dh-raw-03`)*
- **Back-solving from one known sector**: if one sector's real-world value and percentage are both known, that pair gives a unit rate usable to find any other sector's real-world value (Example 1's "15% = ₹3000, so 10% = ₹2000" step). *(`dh-raw-03`, Example 1)*
- **Multi-item central-angle table**: for data with more than two categories, compute each category's fraction of the total, then its angle, in one table (Example 2, bakery sales). *(`dh-raw-03`, Example 2)*

## Section 3 — Chance and probability
- **Random experiment**: an experiment whose outcome cannot be predicted or controlled in advance (e.g. a coin toss). *(`dh-raw-06`)*
- **Outcome**: a single possible result of an experiment. *(`dh-raw-06`)*
- **Event**: one outcome, or a collection of outcomes, of an experiment (e.g. "getting an even number" on a die = the collection {2,4,6}). *(`dh-raw-06`)*
- **Equally likely outcomes**: outcomes that each have the same chance of occurring; empirically, their observed frequencies converge as trials increase (shown via the coin-toss frequency table). *(`dh-raw-06`)*
- **Probability formula**: P(event) = (number of outcomes making the event) / (total number of equally likely outcomes). *(`dh-raw-06`)*
- **Complementary framing**: in the source's rain example, P(rain) + P(no rain) = 1 when the two are treated as the only equally likely possibilities — used informally, not named as a "complement rule." *(`dh-raw-06`)*
- **Real-world application context**: exit polls (small distributed sample → population-level estimate) and weather prediction (historical trend data) are the two named real-life uses of probability. *(`dh-raw-06`)*
