# Stage 4 — Learning Objectives: Rational Numbers (expansion)

## Section 1 — What is a rational number?
1. Recognize a rational number as any number expressible as p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.
2. Reduce a rational number to its standard form (positive denominator, no common factor).

## Section 2 — Closure, commutativity and associativity
3. Determine whether rational numbers are closed under addition, subtraction, multiplication and division.
4. Identify commutativity of addition/multiplication for rational numbers, and recognize that subtraction/division are not commutative.
5. Identify associativity of addition/multiplication for rational numbers, and recognize that subtraction/division are not associative.

## Section 3 — Distributivity and the identity elements
6. State the additive identity (0) and multiplicative identity (1) for rational numbers.
7. Apply the distributive property of multiplication over addition to simplify a calculation.

## Section 4 — Negative and reciprocal
8. Find the additive inverse (negative) of a rational number.
9. Find the multiplicative inverse (reciprocal) of a rational number, and recognize that 0 has no reciprocal.

## Section 5 — The number line and rational numbers between two rationals
10. Use the mean method to reason about a rational number's position relative to two others.
11. Find a rational number between two given rational numbers using the mean method, and recognize that infinitely many such numbers exist.
