## Section 1 — What is a rational number?
- **Definition**: a number expressible as p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0. Integers and whole numbers are rational numbers (e.g. 5 = 5/1). *(§1.1)*
- **Standard form**: a rational number is in standard form when its denominator is positive and the numerator and denominator share no common factor other than 1. *(domain knowledge)*
- **Equivalent forms**: the same rational number can be written with different numerators/denominators (e.g. -4/6 = 2/-3 = -2/3); reducing to standard form gives one canonical representation. *(domain knowledge)*

## Section 2 — Closure, commutativity and associativity
- **Closure**: rational numbers are closed under addition, subtraction and multiplication (the result is always a rational number), but not under division (division by 0 is undefined). *(§1.2.1)*
- **Commutativity**: addition and multiplication are commutative for rational numbers (a+b=b+a, a×b=b×a); subtraction and division are not. *(§1.2.2)*
- **Associativity**: addition and multiplication are associative for rational numbers (a+(b+c)=(a+b)+c, a×(b×c)=(a×b)×c); subtraction and division are not. *(§1.2.3)*
- Whole numbers and integers follow the same closure/commutativity/associativity pattern as rational numbers for addition and multiplication, but integers are also closed under subtraction (whole numbers are not). *(§1.2.1–1.2.3)*

## Section 3 — Distributivity and the identity elements
- **Additive identity**: 0 is the additive identity for rational numbers (and for integers and whole numbers): a + 0 = 0 + a = a. *(§1.2.4)*
- **Multiplicative identity**: 1 is the multiplicative identity for rational numbers: a × 1 = 1 × a = a. *(§1.2.5)*
- **Distributivity**: for all rational numbers a, b, c: a(b+c) = ab+ac and a(b−c) = ab−ac. This lets a calculation be regrouped into an easier form before multiplying. *(§1.2.6)*

## Section 4 — Negative and reciprocal
- **Additive inverse (negative)**: for a rational number a, its additive inverse is −a, satisfying a + (−a) = 0. Every rational number has exactly one additive inverse. *(domain knowledge)*
- **Multiplicative inverse (reciprocal)**: for a non-zero rational number a = p/q, its reciprocal is q/p, satisfying a × (q/p) = 1. **0 has no reciprocal**, since no number multiplied by 0 gives 1. *(domain knowledge)*

## Section 5 — The number line and rational numbers between two rationals
- **Number line representation**: rational numbers occupy positions on the number line between and around the integers, found by subdividing the unit interval according to the denominator. *(domain knowledge)*
- **Mean method**: given two rational numbers a and b, their mean (a+b)/2 is a rational number that lies exactly between them. Repeating this process between any resulting pair produces another rational number between them, which shows that infinitely many rational numbers lie between any two given rational numbers — unlike integers, where only finitely many (or no) integers lie between two given ones. *(domain knowledge, referenced in the chapter's own summary)*
